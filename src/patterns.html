Four months in: the patterns I keep hearing

Since April I’ve been listening to Alexandria’s police and fire dispatch and mapping every pedestrian, cyclist, and scooter hit by a driver — along with crashes on the Braddock Road and Mount Vernon Avenue corridors. The map is meant to speak for itself. But four months in, a handful of patterns have turned up often enough that I wanted to put them in one place for the people who work on this: T&ES and fellow advocates.

Read this as a floor, not a ceiling

Dispatch captures what someone called 911 about — before the crash-report filters that keep most minor strikes out of the official record. So it sees incidents the Vision Zero dashboard never will. It also misses everything no one called in, which is why the real number is always higher than what’s on the map.

One honest note for anyone reading the corridor counts: I only capture vehicle crashes on Braddock Road and Mount Vernon Avenue, so those two streets will always look busier than the rest simply because that’s where I’m looking. The number that isn’t biased by where I look is people struck — which I flag citywide. That distinction matters below.

Eisenhower East is the clearest cluster

Of everywhere on the map, the tightest concentration of people hit is the Eisenhower East pocket around Hoffman Street and Mandeville Lane — five strikes since April, and unlike the corridor counts, nothing about that number is an artifact of where I’m looking. Two scooter riders on Mill Road in the spring, then three pedestrians in the Hoffman–Mandeville blocks over five weeks this summer, two of them hit-and-runs. It’s a small area absorbing a lot of harm.

The Braddock Road corridor, west and east

West end — near the ACHS Minnie Howard campus. A run of crashes at West Braddock Road and North Radford Street in May, and in June a student hit by a driver while crossing at Marlee Way. When the people getting hurt on a corridor include a kid walking to a high school, that’s the corridor’s design talking.

East end — the case for the Braddock Road project, written in dispatch calls. Nothing has changed on the ground here yet, but the crash history the project is meant to fix is already on the record: two pedestrians struck near the Braddock Metro crosswalk in April, four crashes at the Russell Road corner in a single month, two DASH buses hit, and a road-rage crash at Myrtle Street. When the work gets built, this is the stretch it’s answering.

Three patterns that aren’t about one corner

Hit-and-run is routine, not rare

One in four incidents is a hit-and-run — 25 of 112 — and more than half of those are a person struck and left in the road. It shows up everywhere on the map, in every neighborhood, on corridors and side streets alike. The Mandeville strike above is one; there are two dozen more.

DASH buses keep getting hit

Five times in four months a driver hit a DASH bus — on Braddock Road, on Commonwealth, on Mount Vernon Avenue — and in several the driver fled. It’s a distinct pattern I didn’t expect to see, and one DASH and T&ES might both want a closer look at.

▶ Commonwealth Ave & E Braddock Rd · DASH bus July 15
“The crash, property damage, at Commonwealth and Braddock — involving a DASH bus and an SUV. DASH supervisors en route.”

Mount Vernon isn’t uniquely dangerous — King and Duke match it

On the one measure that isn’t biased by where I look — people actually struck — King Street (7), Mount Vernon Avenue (6), and Duke Street (6) are essentially tied. Mount Vernon only looks worse because it’s one of the two streets where I also count every vehicle crash. If the city tracked crashes on King and Duke the way it could, I suspect they’d look just as busy. It’s the strongest argument I’ve got for widening the lens beyond two corridors.
